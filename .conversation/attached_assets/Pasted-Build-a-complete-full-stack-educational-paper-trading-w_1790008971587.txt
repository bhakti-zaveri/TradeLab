Build a complete full-stack educational paper-trading web application called TradeLab.

TradeLab is not a real brokerage and must not provide buy/sell recommendations. It is a simulator that gives users virtual money, lets them replay stored historical market data, place simulated trades, maintain a trade journal, and understand their trading behaviour through analytics.

Use:
- React with TypeScript for the frontend.
- Node.js, Express and TypeScript for the backend.
- Tailwind CSS.
- shadcn/ui-style reusable components.
- Lucide icons.
- Recharts or Apache ECharts for analytics.
- Lightweight Charts or another candlestick chart library for the replay chart.
- React Hook Form and Zod for validated forms.
- TanStack Query for server data.
- Zustand for small client-side state.
- JWT authentication.
- bcrypt or argon2 for password hashing.
- Socket.IO or native WebSocket for the replay price stream.
- Google Sheets API as the persistence layer through the backend. Never expose Google credentials in the browser.
- Keep the data access layer abstract so it can later be replaced by PostgreSQL or Supabase.

The application must work without external live-market APIs. Include a small seed dataset of historical OHLCV candles for a few fictional or educational instruments such as TLAB, NIFTY-DEMO, RELIANCE-DEMO and INFY-DEMO. Clearly label the instruments as simulated educational data.

₹10,00,000.

Replay terminal:
- Use a dark blue/charcoal theme, not pure black.
- Three-column desktop layout:
  - Left: instrument and replay controls.
  - Centre: candlestick chart.
  - Right: order ticket and account summary.
- Support play, pause, next candle, restart and speeds of 1x, 2x, 5x and 10x.
- Display simulated replay date and time.
- At each candle update:
  - Update current price.
  - Revalue open positions.
  - Check pending limit orders.
  - Check stop-loss orders.
  - Update unrealised P&L.
  - Add equity snapshot.
  - Emit the state through WebSocket.
- Provide a working fallback polling mode if WebSocket is unavailable.

Order types:
1. Market order:
   - Execute at the current simulated price.
2. Limit order:
   - Buy limit triggers when current price is less than or equal to limit price.
   - Sell limit triggers when current price is greater than or equal to limit price.
3. Stop-loss:
   - Support long positions first.
   - A long stop-loss triggers when current price is less than or equal to stop price.

Order validation:
- Quantity must be a positive whole number.
- Buy orders require sufficient available cash.
- Sell orders require sufficient shares.
- Limit and stop prices must be valid.
- A stop-loss must be below the entry price for a long position.
- Do not allow orders after the replay session ends.
- Show clear, human-readable validation messages.

After an order is executed:
- Open a mandatory journal modal.
- Require a reason tag:
  - Breakout
  - News
  - Trend continuation
  - Support/resistance
  - Mean reversion
  - FOMO
  - Revenge trade
  - Social media tip
  - Experiment
  - Other
- Allow:
  - Mistake tag
  - Confidence from 1 to 5
  - Planned entry
  - Planned stop
  - Planned target
  - Free-text notes
- Mark a trade as needing review if the journal entry is incomplete.

Calculate and display:

- Winning trades.

- Win rate.

- Average loss.

- Profit factor.
- Largest win.
- Largest loss.
- Average holding time.
- Maximum drawdown.
- Average position size.
- Percentage of trades with planned stops.
- P&L by reason tag.
- P&L by mistake tag.
- Win rate by instrument.
- Equity curve.
- Daily P&L chart.
- Drawdown chart.
- Portfolio allocation chart.

Use these formulas:
winRate = winningTrades / closedTrades * 100
averageWin = totalWinningPnl / winningTrades
averageLoss = totalLosingPnl / losingTrades
riskReward = averageWin / abs(averageLoss)
drawdown = (currentEquity - previousPeakEquity) / previousPeakEquity
maxDrawdown = minimum drawdown value

Add educational behavioural insight cards such as:
- “Your FOMO trades have a lower win rate than your breakout trades.”
- “Your average loss is larger than your average win.”
- “You exceeded your selected risk level in several trades.”
- “Your strongest results came from a particular reason tag.”

These insights must describe the user’s own historical data. They must not make market predictions or give financial advice.
